REVIEW OF RELATED LITERATURE AND SYSTEMS  
Related Literature 
Appointment Schedule 
Appointment scheduling is an important part of healthcare service management 
because it helps organize patient visits according to available healthcare services. Gupta 
and Denton (2020), “The Problem of Appointment Scheduling in Outpatient Clinics: A 
Multiple Case Study of Clinical Practice,” examined appointment scheduling in outpatient 
clinical practice. Miller (2021), “Synchronization of Provider Availability in Digital Health 
Platforms,” focused on coordinating provider availability within digital health platforms. 
Ata and Chen (2022), “Ap pointment Scheduling Problem in Complexity Systems of 
Healthcare Services: A Comprehensive Review,” reviewed appointment scheduling 
problems in healthcare services, while Kammrath Betancor et al. (2025), “Efficient Patient 
Care in the Digital Age: Impact of Online Appointment Scheduling,” examined online 
appointment scheduling and its role in healthcare service delivery. 
These studies provide concepts that support the appointment scheduling component 
of E -KALUSUGAN. The proposed system allows patients to book dental consultations 
online while authorized staff can manage scheduled appointments and dentist availability. 
This provides a digital approach for organizing dental appointments at the Bago City 
Primary Care Facility. 
Walk-in Appointments 
Patient queuing is an important consideration in healthcare facilities because the 
number and timing of patient arrivals can affect waiting time and patient flow. Abubakar et 
al. (2020), “Application of Queuing Theory and Management of Waiting Time Using 
Multiple Server Model,” discussed the application of queuing theory in managing patient 
waiting time using a multiple -server model. Agnihotri and Taylor (2021), “Impact of 
Application of Queuing Theory on Operational Efficiency of Patient Registration,”

Influx: Hybrid Queuing Models for Scheduled and Walk -in Patients,” discussed queuing

“Transparency in Public Health Service Flow: The Role of Digital Queuing,” examined

These concepts support the smart queuing feature of E -KALUSUGAN, which

 Doctor Availability 
Provider availability is an important consideration when managing healthcare 
appointments because appointment schedules depend on the availability of healthcare 
professionals. Gupta and Denton (2020), “Managing Provider Shifts and Real -Time 
Availability in Clinical Settings,” discussed the management of provider shifts and real -
time availability in clinical settings. Williams (2021), “Real -time Availability Tracking: 
Reducing Scheduling Conflicts in Primary Care,” examined real -time availability tracking 
in primary care, while Ata and Chen (2022), “Complexities of Doctor -Patient 
Synchronization in Digital Systems,” discussed the coordination between healthcare 
providers and patients in digital systems. Roberts (2023), “The Impact of Automated 
Schedule Updates on Healthcare Staff Productivity,” examined automated schedule updates 
and their effect on healthcare staff productivity. 
These studies support the dentist availability feature of E -KALUSUGAN. The 
system allows authorized staff to update and monitor the availability of dentists and use this 
information when managing dental appointments. This provides a more organized way of 
coordinating dentist availability with scheduled dental consultations.

Predictive Analytics   
Predictive analytics uses existing data to identify patterns and provide information 
about possible future conditions. Chen, Mao, and Liu (2021), “Big Data Predictive 
Analytics Framework for Forecasting Patient Demand,” discussed a predictive analytics 
framework for patient demand using healthcare data. Wang (2023), “Forecasting Healthcare 
Demand: A Hybrid Predictive Model,” examined predictive modeling for healthcare 
demand, while Lee (2024), “Machine Learning Applications in Public Health Facility 
Resource Planning,” discussed the use of machine learning in public health facility resource 
planning. Kammrath Betancor et al. (2025), “Efficient Patient Care in the Digital Age: 
Predicting No -show Behavior,” examined predictive methods related to patient 
appointment behavior. 
These studies provide a basis for the predictive analytics component of E -
KALUSUGAN. The system will use available historical dental data to predict future patient 
volume according to age group. The resulting information may assist the dental department 
in anticipating changes in patient volume and planning its services. 
SMS Integration 
SMS-based notifications provide a way for healthcare systems to communicate 
appointment and queue information through mobile phones. Prasad et al. (2021), 
“Automated Notification Systems for Queue Management,” discussed automated 
notifications for managing patient queues. Fadhil (2022), “The Role of SMS Reminders in 
Reducing No-Show Rates in Public Health,” examined the use of SMS reminders in public 
health appointments. Kumar (2022), “Efficiency of Automated SMS Alerts in Real -time 
Queuing Systems,” discussed automated SMS alerts for real-time queue management, while 
Gomez (2023), “Closing the Digital Divide: SMS Solutions for Public Health 
Accessibility,” examined SMS solutions for public health accessibility.

Appointment Scheduling 
Web-based appointment systems provide healthcare facilities with a way to 
organize patient appointments and manage appointment-related information. Tufail (2020),

Northwestern University (2022), “Medical Appointment Scheduling System: Capstone 
Study,” presented a digital system for medical appointment management, while OpenEMR 
Project (2023), “Open-Source Electronic Health Record and Medical Practice Management 
Solution,” provided an open -source platform for electronic health records and medical 
practice management. 
These systems provide a basis for the appointment scheduling component of E -
KALUSUGAN. The proposed system allows patients to book dental consultations online 
while authorized staff manage scheduled appointments and dentist availability. E -
KALUSUGAN applies these appointment management concepts specifically to the dental 
services of the Bago City Primary Care Facility.

Synthesis/Research Gap 
The related reviewed literature and related systems show that digital technologies 
can support different healthcare service processes, including appointment scheduling, 
patient queuing, provider availability, predictive analytics, and SMS -based notifications. 
The reviewed appointment scheduling studies and systems demonstrate the use of web -
based platforms to organize healthcare appointments, while queuing systems provide 
approaches for managing scheduled and walk-in patients and monitoring patient flow. Other 
systems focus on provider availability, predictive analytics using historical healthcare data, 
and SMS-based communication for appointment and queue updates. 
However, the reviewed systems generally focus on specific healthcare functions 
rather than combining these functions according to the workflow of a local public primary 
care facility. The reviewed predictive analytics systems also address registration volu me, 
while the proposed study focuses specifically on predicting dental patient volume according 
to age group. Furthermore, the reviewed SMS -based systems demonstrate the use of text 
messages for healthcare communication, but E -KALUSUGAN prepares this funct ion as 
part of a broader dental service management platform, with actual SMS delivery subject to 
service provider integration. 
To address these gaps, E -KALUSUGAN combines online dental appointment 
scheduling, structured queuing for scheduled and walk -in patients, dentist availability
