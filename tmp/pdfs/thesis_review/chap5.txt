CHAPTER V 
CONCLUSION AND RECOMMENDATION 
This chapter presents the drawn conclusions and recommendations relevant to the present 
study. 
Conclusion 
 E-KALUSUGAN: A Web-Based Appointment Scheduling System for Bago City Primary 
Care Facility with Predictive Analytics was developed to improve the management of dental 
services at the Bago City Primary Care Facility. The system is designed for four types of  users, 
namely patients, staff, dentists, and administrators, with each user having specific functions 
according to their responsibilities. Patients can schedule dental consultations, join the queue, and 
view their appointment details and queue status. Sta ff can manage appointments, queue tickets, 
dentist availability, and patient records. Dentists can review patient information, manage their 
availability, conduct consultations, and monitor the queue. Meanwhile, administrators can manage 
user accounts, maintain system settings, oversee records, and access administrative functions. E -
KALUSUGAN integrates appointment scheduling, queue management, dentist availability 
monitoring, centralized patient and appointment records, and predictive analytics within one web-
based platform. The predictive analytics component uses historical dental data and the ARIMA 
model to predict annual patient volume according to age group, providing information that may 
assist the facility in planning its dental services. The system al so includes a prepared SMS 
notification component for future integration with an SMS service provider. 
 The developed system is intended to address the limitations of manual procedures used in 
managing dental appointments, queue tickets, dentist availability, and patient information. By 
bringing these functions together in one platform, E -KALUSUGAN aims to r educe reliance on 
separate manual records, improve the organization of scheduled and walk -in patients, and make 
appointment and queue information easier to access. Its predictive analytics feature also provides 
a data-based approach to anticipating annual dental patient volume by age group. The system is 
designed with a mobile-responsive interface so users can access its features through smartphones, 
tablets, and computers. Its queue management functions accommodate both scheduled and walk -
in patients while allowing staff to monitor patient flow throughout the dental service process. In

Recommendation 
After developing and evaluating E -KALUSUGAN: A Web -Based Appointment 
Scheduling System for Bago City Primary Care Facility with Predictive Analytics, the proponents 
recommend the following: 
1. To improve the system's notification capabilities, the proponents recommend integrating 
E-KALUSUGAN with a reliable SMS service provider. This would enable the actual 
delivery of appointment confirmations, reminders, and queue updates to help patients stay 
informed about their dental consultations.

2. The proponents recommend implementing E -KALUSUGAN at the Bago City Primary 
Care Facility as the initial deployment for its dental services. After completing the 
necessary testing, evaluation, and approval, the facility may use the system to support 
appointment scheduling, queue management, dentist availability monitoring, and patient 
record management. 
3. Training programs should be provided for patients, staff, dentists, and administrators, 
supported by user manuals and clear instructions. These materials would help users 
understand the system's features and perform their respective tasks more effectively. 
4. Having a dedicated technical support team or assigned personnel could help address 
technical issues, maintain system functionality, and assist users when problems occur. 
Regular maintenance and updates would also help ensure that the system continues to meet 
the operational needs of the facility. 
5. The predictive analytics component could be improved by collecting additional historical 
dental records and evaluating the ARIMA model using appropriate performance metrics. 
Future researchers may also compare other forecasting methods to determine which 
approach provides more accurate predictions of annual dental patient volume according to 
age group.
