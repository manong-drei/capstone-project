METHODOLOGY 
Research Design 
 This study employs a Developmental Research Design to develop and implement 
E-KALUSUGAN: A Web-Based Appointment Scheduling System for Bago City Primary 
Care Facility with Predictive Analytics. This research design is appropriate because the 
study focuses on creating a functional web-based system that addresses the identified needs 
in dental appointment scheduling, patient queuing, dentist availability, patient records, and 
patient volume prediction. 
The development process involves identifying the requirements of the Bago City 
Primary Care Facility, designing the system based on these requirements, developing its 
features, and testing the system to determine whether it functions according to the intended 
specifications. The system will be developed using React for the frontend, Node.js and 
Express.js for the backend, and MySQL for database management. Predictive analytics will 
also be incorporated using available historical dental data to generate pati ent volume 
predictions according to age group. 
The developmental design also allows the proponents to evaluate the system after 
its implementation and determine whether its features support the intended dental service 
processes. The system will undergo testing and evaluation to identify functional issu es, 
usability concerns, and areas for improvement before its final implementation. This process 
provides an opportunity to refine the system based on the results of testing and feedback 
from its intended users. 
The completed system will then be evaluated based on its functionality and ability 
to support the dental service processes of the facility. Feedback and results from the 
evaluation will be used to identify areas that may require improvement. Through this 
design, the study follows a systematic process of developing and accessing a technology-
based solution suited to the identified needs of the Bago City Primary Care Facility.

System Development Methodology 
This study uses the Agile methodology as the system development methodology for 
E-KALUSUGAN: A Web-Based Appointment Scheduling System for Bago City Primary 
Care Facility with Predictive Analytics. Agile is suitable for the study because it supports 
an iterative and flexible approach, allowing the proponents to develop, test, review, and 
improve the system based on identified requirements and feedback. The methodology will 
be carried out through seven phases: Plan, Design, Develop, Test, Deploy, Review, and  
Launch. The proponents will begin by planning the project requirements and objectives, 
followed by designing the system structure and interface. The system will then be 
developed and tested to verify its functions, prepared for deployment, reviewed based on 
testing and evaluation results, and launched once the necessary improvements have been 
completed.

Machine Learning Methodology 
 E-KALUSUGAN uses Autoregressive Integrated Moving Average (ARIMA) 
timeseries models to forecast annual dental service recipient counts by age group, 
supporting dental service planning at the Bago City Primary Care Facility.  
The pipeline includes dataset preparation, validation, chronological organization, 
model selection, fitting, testing, and forecasting. Annual data are loaded from a CSV file 
and checked for required attributes, valid numerical values, duplicates, and compl ete 
reporting years, then organized chronologically into seven age-group time series.  
For each series, the successfully converged ARIMA candidate with the lowest 
Akaike Information Criterion (AIC) is selected. Performance is assessed through expanding 
window evaluation, using earlier observations to predict the following year. Final models 
use all observations from 2014–2025 to forecast 2026. Forecasts, prediction intervals, and 
evaluation results are exported as CSV files for the system’s analytics interface.  
Dataset 
The forecasting component uses a CSV dataset of annual dental service recipient 
counts covering 2014–2025. It contains 84 aggregated records: twelve annual observations 
for each of seven age groups. Each record represents one year and age group rather than an 
individual patient. Bago City Primary Care Facility provided the actual dataset for 2020 – 
2025, comprising 42 records. The remaining 42 records for 2014 –2019 are explicitly 
identified as synthetic placeholders rather than original facility records.  
The seven age groups are infants aged 0 –11 months, children aged 1 –4 years, 
children aged 5 –9 years, adolescents aged 10 –14 years, adolescents aged 15 –19 years, 
adults aged 20–59 years, and senior citizens aged 60 years and above.

The dataset contains thirteen attributes: period start, reporting year, reporting 
frequency, age group, age group order, estimated population, male recipients, female 
recipients, total recipients, data source, synthetic -data indicator, synthetic generation  
method, and data quality note. Total recipients are the forecasting variable; reporting year 
establishes chronology, and age group identifies each separate time series. Other attributes 
document demographic context, ordering, and data provenance. Estimated  population and 
sex-disaggregated counts are not predictors in the current ARIMA implementation.  
The dataset contains no patient names, contact information, or individual medical 
records. Its annual frequency supports yearly recipient forecasts rather than direct 
predictions of daily queues or monthly demand.  
Data Processing 
Data preprocessing begins with checking the presence of the required attributes:  
reporting year, age group, age group order, total recipients, data source, and the synthetic 
data indicator. Reporting years are converted to integers; total recipient counts are 
converted to numerical values, and synthetic data indicators are converted to  Boolean 
values.  
The implementation checks for missing recipient totals, invalid synthetic data 
indicators, negative recipient counts, and duplicate combinations of reporting year and age 
groups. Records that fail these checks cause processing to stop so that the dataset c an be 
reviewed and corrected. Missing target values are not automatically replaced with zero or 
estimated through imputation.  
Each age group is checked to ensure that it contains one observation for every year 
from 2014 to 2025. The implementation also verifies that observations for 2014 –2019 are 
labeled synthetic and that observations for 2020 –2025 are labeled actual. The validated 
records are sorted by reporting year and organized into separate chronological series. The 
model uses recipient counts in their original units, and separate models are constructed for 
each age group. Unusually high or low annual counts remain in the series rather than being 
deleted without supporting evidence.

ML Algorithm Used 
The forecasting algorithm implemented in E -KALUSUGAN is ARIMA, 
represented as ARIMA(p, d, q). The parameter p specifies the autoregressive order, d 
specifies the differencing order, and q specifies the moving-average order.   
  
The autoregressive component relates an observation to preceding values, 
differencing models changes between observations, and the moving -average component 
accounts for preceding model errors.  
The implementation uses the ARIMA functionality provided by the Python stats 
models library. Eight candidate configurations are evaluated by allowing each parameter to 
take a value of zero or one. This produces configurations ranging from ARIMA(0, 0, 0) to  
ARIMA(1, 1, 1). The limited candidate set keeps model selection manageable for the short 
annual series.  
Candidate models that fail to fit or do not converge are excluded from selection. 
Among the remaining candidates, the configuration with the lowest AIC is selected. A 
separate model is fitted for each age group.  
The implemented models are nonseasonal and do not include external predictors. 
Consequently, they forecast annual recipient totals rather than seasonal appointment 
patterns or demand for individual dental procedures. Forecasts and interval limits are 
rounded to whole numbers, and negative output values are restricted to zero.  
Model Training and Testing 
Model training and testing use an expanding window time -series evaluation. The 
chronological order of observations is preserved, and each validation year is predicted using 
only observations from preceding years. This approach provides four one -year-ahead 
validation rounds for each age group.

At every round, candidate models are fitted using the training period, and the lowest 
AIC configuration is selected. The selected model predicts the recipient count for the 
validation year, which is then compared with that year’s recorded count. Across seven age 
groups, this procedure produces 28 validation predictions.  
The evaluation follows an expanding -window form of time -series cross-validation 
rather than random splitting or ordinary shuffled k-fold cross-validation. Model selection is 
repeated within each training window, without using the corresponding validation -year 
outcome to select the configuration.  
A naive forecasting baseline is also included. This baseline predicts that the next 
year’s recipient count will equal the preceding year’s count. Its errors provide a reference 
for assessing whether ARIMA offers an improvement over a simpler forecasting method.  
After testing, a final model for each age group is fitted using all twelve observations 
from 2014–2025 to forecast 2026. The validation outcomes are drawn from years labeled 
actual, but every training window includes six synthetic observations. The evaluation 
therefore describes performance under the current mixed-data setup and does not establish 
forecasting reliability using exclusively authentic historical records.

Performance Evaluation Metrics 
Forecasting performance is evaluated using Mean Absolute Error (MAE), Root 
Mean Squared Error (RMSE) and Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE). These metrics 
are calculated separately for each age group using the validation predictions for 2022–2025.  
Mean Absolute Error. MAE measures the average absolute difference between the 
recorded and predicted recipient counts:  
MAE = Σ|actual − predicted| / n  
MAE expresses the typical forecast error in recipient counts. A lower value indicates 
a smaller average error.  
Root Mean Squared Error.  RMSE is calculated by taking the square root of the 
average squared forecast error:  
RMSE = √[Σ(actual − predicted)² / n]  
RMSE gives greater weight to larger errors and helps identify models that occasionally 
produce substantial deviations from recorded counts.

Security Architecture 
  The E -Kalusugan System implements a robust, two -tiered security framework 
dividing responsibilities between frontend user interaction and backend data processing to 
safeguard sensitive health records. At the client level, the Frontend Security Layer handles 
initial user protection through client -side form validation, role -guarded route navigation, 
secure token handling, session lifecycle management, dynamic XSS sanitization, and 
graceful error handling. Once requests reach the server, the Backend Security Layer 
enforces strict data integrity via an API gateway, JWT -based authentication middleware, 
fine-grained RoleBased Access Control (RBAC), rate limiting, parameterized database 
queries, comprehensive audit logging, and full HTTPS/TLS transport encryption before 
data is safely stored.

Performance Evaluation. Using Playwright and Black Box Testing 
The user interface of the developed system will be tested using Playwright and the 
Black Box Testing approach. Playwright will be utilized as an automated testing tool to 
simulate user interactions with the web application in a browser environment. Automat ed 
test scenarios will be created to evaluate common user activities such as logging into the 
system, navigating between pages, entering information into forms, clicking buttons, 
submitting records, and verifying displayed results or system responses. Black Box Testing 
will be used as the primary testing approach in evaluating the functionality of the user 
interface. This approach focuses on testing the behavior of the system based on specified 
inputs and expected outputs without considering its internal so urce code or program 
structure. Test cases will be developed based on the functional requirements and major 
features of the system. Each test case will contain the test scenario, test input or user action, 
expected result, actual result, and test status. A test case will be marked as passed when the 
actual result corresponds with the expected result; otherwise, it will be recorded as failed 
and subjected to further correction and retesting. 
The use of Playwright together with Black Box Testing will provide a systematic 
method for identifying functional errors, form validation issues, navigation problems, 
incorrect system responses, and other user interface -related defects before the final 
implementation of the system.

Admin Dashboard 
 The E -Kalusugan Administrator Dashboard serves as the central management 
interface for overseeing the system’s users, doctors, patients, and healthcare activities. It 
provides the administrator with access to doctor profiles and availability, patient histo ry, 
and account management for staff and patients. The dashboard also allows the administrator 
to add, edit, deactivate, and monitor user accounts, helping ensure that system information 
remains organized and up to date. Through features such as the Queue Monitor,

administrator can effectively monitor and manage the overall operation of the E-Kalusugan

Registration Dashboard 
 The E -Kalusugan Staff Dashboard provides staff members with an organized 
interface for managing patients and the queue. It allows staff to select the type of service, 
such as Dental Check -up or General Consultation, enter the patient’s information, and 
assign a regular or priority queue. The dashboard also displays the currently served patient 
and the next patient in line, helping staff manage patient flow efficiently. Through the Get 
Queue and Next Queue functions, staff can register patients and call the next patient for 
service, making the E -Kalusugan appointment and queue management process faster and 
more organized.

Predictive Analytics 
 The E-Kalusugan Analytics Dashboard provides administrators and authorized staff 
with an overview of dental service data through visual reports and predictive analytics. It 
presents patient volume and historical dental records to help users understand patient trends 
and monitor service demand. The dashboard also includes predictive analytics using the 
ARIMA model to predict annual dental patient volume according to age group. These 
insights can assist the Bago City Primary Care Facility in understanding pat ient demand
